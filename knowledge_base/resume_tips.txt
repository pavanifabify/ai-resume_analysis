Resume Writing Best Practices

Structuring Bullet Points
Use the format: Action Verb + Task + Result/Impact (quantified where possible).
Weak: "Responsible for managing a team."
Strong: "Led a team of 5 engineers to deliver a payments migration 3 weeks ahead of
schedule, reducing transaction latency by 40%."

Strong action verbs by category:
- Leadership: Led, Directed, Coordinated, Mentored, Managed
- Building/Creating: Built, Designed, Developed, Architected, Implemented
- Improving: Optimized, Streamlined, Automated, Reduced, Increased
- Analysis: Analyzed, Evaluated, Diagnosed, Investigated
Avoid weak, passive phrasing like "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with" -
these describe duties, not accomplishments.

Quantifying Impact
Whenever possible, attach a number: percentage improvement, time saved, users
affected, revenue impact, dataset size, latency reduced, test coverage increased.
If you don't have an exact number, a reasonable, honest estimate ("approximately",
"an estimated") is far better than no number at all - it still tells a reviewer the
scale of what you did. Never fabricate metrics.

Student and Fresher Resumes
- Lead with Education and Skills, not a thin Experience section.
- Projects should be the centerpiece: 3-5 well-described projects outperform a padded
  experience section with vague internships.
- Include coursework, hackathons, and open-source contributions - these substitute
  for professional experience and show initiative.
- A one-page resume is almost always correct at this stage. Recruiters spend seconds,
  not minutes, on early screens.

Experienced Professional Resumes
- Lead with a concise professional summary (2-3 lines) tailored to the target role.
- Order experience reverse-chronologically, with the most recent/relevant role having
  the most detail (4-6 bullets); older roles can be condensed to 2-3 bullets.
- Emphasize business impact and leadership, not just technical tasks.
- Two pages is acceptable for 7+ years of experience; otherwise, aim for one page.

Project Description Quality
A strong project description answers: What problem did this solve? What was the
technical approach/stack? What was the outcome or scale? A weak description just
lists a tech stack ("Built with React, Node.js, MongoDB") without explaining what
the project does or why it matters.

Common Resume Mistakes
- Generic objective statements that add no information ("Seeking a challenging role
  to utilize my skills").
- Listing every technology ever touched instead of curating to what's relevant and
  genuinely proficient.
- Inconsistent tense (mixing past and present tense for current vs. past roles).
- Typos and grammar errors - these are disproportionately damaging because they signal
  lack of attention to detail, which is core to most technical roles.
- Unexplained employment gaps longer than 6 months - a brief, honest one-line
  explanation is better than silence.

Formatting for Readability
- Use consistent spacing, bullet style, and date alignment throughout.
- Bold job titles or company names (not both) to create visual hierarchy without clutter.
- Limit each bullet to 1-2 lines; if it wraps to a third line, it's usually too long.
- White space matters - a dense, cramped resume is harder to skim than one with
  breathing room, even if it means being more selective about content.
